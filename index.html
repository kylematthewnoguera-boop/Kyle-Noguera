01 / A little introduction

A clinician with an expansive point of view.

I am a Filipino licensed occupational therapist working across pediatric and adult rehabilitation settings, with experience in hospital-based, private-clinic, home-based, and school-based care. My approach values meaningful participation, careful listening, evidence-informed decisions, and partnership with clients, families, and interdisciplinary teams.

I earned my B.S. in Occupational Therapy at the University of Santo Tomas and became a licensed OT in 2024. I am currently pursuing my M.S. in Occupational Therapy at UST, exploring how practice, research, and lived experiences connect.

Outside professional practice, I compete nationally in Standard Ballroom dancesport, love discovering unfamiliar places, and cherish the everyday world of my cats, Tsuki and Hoshi. I want this space to reflect all the meaningful roles that make me who I am.

“There is no single way to live a meaningful life—and no single path through this profession.”

Professional reflections

The road less linear

There is no single way to build a meaningful career in occupational therapy.

Read the full reflection ↗

I entered occupational therapy knowing that I was drawn to the extraordinary breadth of the profession. From the beginning, I hoped to work across ages, settings, and occupations rather than draw a boundary around only one kind of practice.

My practice has taken me through pediatric clinics, hospital-based care, adult rehabilitation, school participation, and the small everyday routines that matter so much to the people we work with. Each setting keeps teaching me a different version of the same lesson: participation is personal. What is important in one context may be completely different in another.

Continuing my graduate studies is part of that same curiosity. I want to learn why we practice the way we do, where the evidence is pointing, and how our profession can keep widening its imagination without losing sight of the person in front of us.

I am learning to see a nonlinear career not as an absence of direction, but as a way of making connections. My commitment is to stay curious, stay grounded in evidence, and make room for many meaningful occupations—my clients’ and my own.

02✦

Movement & Sports OT

A dancer, an athlete, an occupational therapist

What if our roles off the dance floor matter just as much as our performance on it?

Read the full reflection ↗

Standard Ballroom has been one of the most enduring parts of my life. National-level dancesport competition asks for precision, repetition, partnership, focus, and a willingness to begin again after a difficult performance. It is a world of movement, but it is also a world of identity and routine.

For a long time, I kept my life as an athlete and my work in occupational therapy in separate boxes. Exploring Sports Occupational Therapy helped me imagine what it might look like to bring those worlds into the same conversation.

Athletes do not only perform. They recover, study, work, rest, maintain relationships, and make decisions about who they are beyond competition. An occupation-centered lens asks what makes those lives meaningful and sustainable—not merely how to improve one competitive result.

I am still learning and exploring this area of practice, supported by my coursework and continuing education in sports rehabilitation. I am excited by the questions it opens: how can we support participation through injury, transition, balance, performance demands, and changes in athletic identity?

03✦

Practice & advocacy

People before assumptions

A few reflections on listening, language, and neurodiversity-affirming care.

Read the full reflection ↗

Occupational therapy asks us to understand what participation means to the person we are working with. That sounds straightforward until we notice how easily professional assumptions can speak louder than somebody’s own experience.

In my clinical learning and practice, neurodiversity-affirming and trauma-informed approaches have encouraged me to listen more carefully, consider sensory and environmental needs, and collaborate on goals that are meaningful to the individual and their family.

Language is a part of that work. Conversations around identity-first and person-first terminology are not solved by one universal rule. They invite us to ask, listen, and respect how people choose to describe themselves.

I hope to keep bringing that spirit of collaboration into pediatric care, adult care, research, and professional conversations: humility, good evidence, and the recognition that people are experts in important parts of their own lives.

Travel & adventures

Postcards from somewhere new

On getting a little lost, being a beginner, and saying yes to adventures.

Read the full reflection ↗

I love the feeling of entering an unfamiliar place: learning a transit system, noticing the rhythm of a street, finding beauty in ordinary details, and realizing how much there still is to discover.

My travel photographs hold all kinds of moments. There are city lights and train stations, wide landscapes and rainy alleys, quiet flowers under huge skies—and one particularly unforgettable moment skydiving above Dubai.

Travel reminds me that curiosity is a practice. Sometimes it feels adventurous and dramatic. Sometimes it is just reading a sign, asking a question, or admitting that I do not know what comes next.

That openness is something I want to keep carrying with me, both within and beyond my profession.

05✦

Life beyond the clinic

On the little things: Tsuki & Hoshi

A quiet celebration of two very loved cats and the routines that make life feel like home.

Read the full reflection ↗

It is often the smallest parts of a day that stay with us: familiar footsteps, a favorite corner of the room, a moment of stillness, the soft sound of a cat settling nearby.

Tsuki and Hoshi have been deeply loved parts of my life. Their personalities, their companionship, and the ordinary routines around them remind me how meaningful everyday life can be.

As someone who spends so much time thinking about occupations and participation, I appreciate these moments outside professional language. Caring, playing, resting, and simply being together matter.

This little corner of my portfolio is for the tenderness and delight they bring to my story. Some of the most meaningful things in life do not need to be big to be important.
